# Oravan Moments v2 — the live layer (build spec)

**Status:** owner-approved for build, 2026-07-25. **Owner:** solo builder; Claude opens PRs, Colby merges. **Constraint stack unchanged:** zero server-side user data, bilingual parity, nonpartisan by construction, AA accessibility, static-first, spend inside the standing <$5/day directive.

---

## 0. One-paragraph shape

A live Moment page grows two things: a **timeline of dated updates** (up to five rendered per day — a cap, not a quota) and a **"Where it stands" state summary** regenerated when the issue actually moves. Updates are *events from the record* — floor actions, roll-call votes, status changes, schedule placements, and press clusters that clear the existing two-outlet guardrail — each carrying the government's verbatim text beside a decoded one-liner in both languages. The whole layer is data in git: written by the existing sync cadence, reviewable by diff, rendered statically. Oravan does not become a newsroom; it becomes the only news surface on the internet where every item is anchored to a votable object one tap from a phone call, and where "nothing happened today" is a first-class, honestly-rendered state.

## 1. The amendment of v1 — said out loud

v1 (2026-07-23-moments.md §2) declared **"a news product"** a structural non-goal: *"No dated update stream… the schema has no update-stream field to fill,"* and §3.4 listed news-product creep as an abuse case whose design answer was that structural absence. **v2 deliberately reverses the mechanism while keeping the goal.** The thing v1 actually feared was *editorial liability* — unaccountable, momentum-flavored, urgency-manufacturing commentary. v2's answer is not absence but **constitution**: a written editorial law (§2), record-anchoring on every government-sourced update (§4), a render cap with an honest overflow line (§3), attribution requirements on everything press-sourced, and git as the audit trail. The non-goal stands as originally intended — no editorializing about momentum, no urgency theater, no "developing" ticker. The enforcement moved from schema-absence to schema-discipline. This paragraph exists so the reversal reads as a decision, not drift.

## 2. The law — every sentence in Oravan's own voice

> **Truth about the record, attribution about the spin.**
>
> When the record speaks, we say it plainly — numbers, dates, tallies, text — even when plainness lands harder on one side. Balance is not achieved by blunting facts.
>
> When the record is silent — motive, likelihood, what it "really" means — Oravan's voice stops, and named sources speak or nobody does. Speculation never wears our voice.

*(Owner-selected wording, 2026-07-25, from three workshopped candidates.)*

**Enforcement annex — how a script obeys a manifesto.** The law is public-facing text; these are its mechanics, all CI-gated in both languages:

1. **Speculation lint on record classes.** Update text for `vote` / `status_change` / `floor_action` / `scheduled` may not contain hedging or forecast constructions (`expected to`, `likely to`, `could`, `may`, `set to`; `se espera`, `probablemente`, `podría`, `estaría`, …). A record claim is stated flatly or not stated.
2. **Attribution requirement on press.** `press_cluster` text must name at least one of its source outlets, in both languages. Non-record claims never appear unattributed.
3. **The inherited vocabulary table.** v1's forbidden-vocabulary lint (advocacy verbs, crisis/attack/scheme, party-as-adversary framing, EN+ES, quoted-title exemption) applies to every update and every summary revision, imported from `lib/moments-gate.mjs` — one table, never copied.
4. **The record ships beside the voice.** Every government-sourced update stores the verbatim action text, codes, and roll-call references it decodes. A wrong decode is falsifiable in one click, and a kill-switch (`MOMENT_UPDATES_VERBATIM=1`) renders the record instead of the voice with no data migration.
5. **What the lint cannot catch, the structure does.** Selection bias in *which* events appear is answered by the event classes being mechanical (a vote is a vote) and press clusters inheriting the lean-diverse two-outlet rule; the file records everything qualified, so the selection is auditable after the fact.

## 3. Cadence honesty — the cap, the quiet day, and the latency we actually have

- **Up to five updates render per day per Moment.** The store keeps *every* qualified event (owner decision: render cap, not write cap — a script silently discarding a roll-call vote because four floor actions preceded it would itself be an editorial act). Rendering selects by class priority — `vote > status_change > floor_action > scheduled > press_cluster` — with a deterministic tie-break, and prints an honest overflow line ("N further recorded actions this day →" linking to the vehicle's Congress.gov actions page) whenever more exist.
- **A quiet day renders as a quiet day** — one ink line, computed at render, never a stored fake update. Today's quiet line reads "nothing recorded *yet* today"; a past day's reads plainly. This is the anti-manufactured-urgency promise carried into a news surface: the site that would rather show you an empty page than pad it.
- **"Nothing moved" vs "we couldn't check" are different sentences.** A client-side staleness sentinel (the existing `StalenessNote` hydration-beacon pattern) appends "we haven't been able to check since {date}" when the visitor's own clock says the pipeline has gone dark. No heartbeat field, no hourly commit, no deploy storm.
- **Latency, stated honestly:** the pipeline is the existing hourly-nominal newsdesk cadence (GitHub throttles it to ~11 runs/day observed) plus CI and deploy. Median event-to-page ≈ **1.5–2 h**, p95 ≈ **4 h**. Copy never claims "live" as an adjective of speed; every surface prints when we last checked. (The word "Live" in "Live Moments" names the *fight being live*, not the feed — the distinction the timestamps make legible.) Latency numbers are re-measured from 30 days of real commits before ever being quoted publicly.

## 4. Data model

New machine-authored **`data/moment-updates.json`**, keyed by moment id. `data/moments.json` stays hand-authored — the ownership split means auto-commits and hand edits never contend for one file.

```jsonc
{
  "_meta": { "schema": 1, "generated_at": "…" },   // NEVER restamped on a no-op run
  "government-funding-deadline": {
    "updates": [
      {
        "id": "u_<fnv1a>",                          // deterministic — see identity rules
        "class": "vote",                            // vote | status_change | floor_action | scheduled | press_cluster | correction
        "vehicle": "hr-9770-119",
        "day": "2026-07-21",                        // the LEGISLATIVE day — actionDate as supplied, ET-derived for clusters
        "occurred_at": "2026-07-21", "occurred_precision": "day",
        "recorded_at": "2026-07-22T02:31:04Z",      // when the pipeline saw it — back-dating is legible, never invisible
        "text": { "en": "…≤160 chars…", "es": "…" },
        "source": { "kind": "congress_actions", "refs": ["…/actions", "https://clerk.house.gov/evs/2026/roll272.xml"] },
        "record": {                                  // null only on press_cluster
          "action_text": "On passage Passed by the Yeas and Nays: 220 - 205 (Roll no. 272)…",
          "action_code": "H37100", "action_type": "Floor", "source_system": "House floor actions",
          "roll_call": { "chamber": "house", "number": 272 }
        },
        "ai": true
      }
    ],
    "summary_revisions": [                           // append-only; current = .at(-1); rendered with dated history
      { "id": "s_…", "generated_at": "…", "as_of_day": "…",
        "text": { "en": "…", "es": "…" },
        "grounded_in": { "vehicle_statuses": {…}, "update_ids": […], "refs": ["https://www.cbo.gov/…"] },
        "changed_because": ["status:hr-9770-119 committee→floor_vote"], "model": "claude-sonnet-5" }
    ]
  }
}
```

**Identity and dedupe (amended after live verification, 2026-07-25).** The Congress.gov actions endpoint returns the same event twice — once from the chamber's source system (`H37100`, carries `actionTime`) and once as a Library of Congress echo (`8000`, "Passed/agreed to in House: …") — with *different* action codes and text. Text-based identity cannot collide them. Therefore: **when `recordedVotes` is present, identity = chamber + roll number** (a roll call uniquely names its event; prefer the chamber-source record, suppress the LOC echo). Voteless actions key on `actionCode` + normalized text, tolerating the null action codes Senate actions carry. Within a vehicle-day, a record-class event suppresses a `scheduled` signal for the same vehicle — the record beats the signal. Ids are FNV-1a over `(momentId, class, vehicle, occurredKey, identityKey)`; the gate module stays import-free.

**The `correction` class.** An update is never silently edited or deleted inside retention. A material error gets a new `correction`-class update referencing the corrected id, rendered with its own label. A news surface without a corrections mechanism isn't one; git history makes even the correction auditable.

**Retention.** 60 days / 200 updates / 30 revisions per moment; retired moments' entries deleted (git history is the archive — a second archive file nothing renders is dead weight). Gate warns at 384 KB file size, fails at 512 KB.

## 6. Generation, spend, arming

- **New `scripts/moment-updates.mjs`** — a separate `continue-on-error` step in the existing `newsdesk.yml` (incremental) and `sync-bills.yml` (nightly: + press clusters + summaries + prune, before `verify-sync.mjs`). `scripts/newsdesk.mjs` is not touched beyond a boundary comment; its self-enforced ownership discipline is the model, not the victim.
- **Update one-liners:** ONE batched Haiku call per run (≤15 events, EN+ES, strict JSON, skipped entirely when the batch is empty). **Summaries:** Sonnet 5, EN+ES in one call, regenerated *only* when something changed (new update, vehicle status change, or a 7-day re-anchor) and always from the record — never edited from the prior revision, which is stored, not fed back.
- **Spend:** typical **+$0.10–0.17/day**, worst $0.40, code-enforced black-swan ceiling **$0.57/day** (`MOMENT_UPDATE_BATCH_CAP=15`, `MOMENT_UPDATE_DAILY_EVENTS=40`, `MOMENT_SUMMARY_DAILY_CAP=8`). No Batch API (an unbounded wait inside a job that must finish before verify-sync); no prompt caching (both prompts sit under the models' minimum cacheable prefixes — commented in code so nobody "optimizes" it later). Congress.gov adds ~290 free requests/day. Actions minutes are free (public repo). Expect +2–4 data commits/day → Vercel Pro build minutes, watched.
- **Armed from day one** (owner decision, 2026-07-25, chosen over a dark-launch review period): AI text flows from the first merged slice. Safety net: the verbatim kill-switch (§2.4), the gate linting both languages mechanically, and the unreviewed-Spanish risk explicitly routed to the ~Aug 2026 Spanish-reviewer round.
- **Constitutional posture, stated because it is the nearest this feature comes to the line:** "AI content is human-reviewed before it drives a call." An update never drives a call — the CTA remains exclusively on the hand-authored vehicle cards. This is inside the letter of the rule; saying so in the PR body and a header comment keeps it inside the spirit.

## 7. Page anatomy (the amendments to v1 §4.2)

Order on `/moments/[id]`: name and badges → the hand-authored **"What Congress is deciding"** summary **first** (the issue front-and-center; dated motion subordinate) → **"Where it stands — {date}"** (AI chip at first contact, disclaimer, `<details>` revision history with `changed_because` lines) → **"What's moved"** timeline (grouped by legislative day, ≤5 rendered + overflow, quiet days rendered honestly) → vehicles (unchanged) → why-this-exists + `context_refs` row → browse-all. Design laws hold: no new amber (a `scheduled` update prints its date in ink; amber stays the vehicle card's one dated floor fact), no new green, ink carries the section, one AI chip per section. `/moments` joins the header nav and the sitemap — the surface is promoted from experiment to flagship.

**Privacy copy direction** (threaded through, not a banner): the sharp line is *"every other news source is watching you read it."* No login, no tracking, no personalization is what makes the same page shareable across the spectrum — nobody's feed customized it. One succinct line on the moments index; the sentiment recurs in the summary-footer disclosure and the existing footer.
